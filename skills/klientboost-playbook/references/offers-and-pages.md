# Offers and page architecture

Use this when building the site IA, a lead magnet, pricing narrative, or a service/results page.

## The offer

**Product:** a custom marketing plan positioned as a $10k deliverable, given away.

**What it contains (show samples on the page):**

- ICP cards with firmographics, jobs-to-be-done, and the KPI that person actually watches
- Mocked ads and landing pages for those ICPs
- Top competitors with ad-platform overlap, creative volume, paid-keyword overlap
- Highest-opportunity keywords not yet targeted (volume, CPC, opportunity)
- Native audiences per platform, labeled **demand gen vs demand capture**
- SEO leftovers competitors are sleeping on
- Competitor A/B tests they claim to watch
- Where those ICPs already hang out (podcasts, newsletters, YouTube) with cost
- CRM stage conversion assumptions
- **Growth grid:** ranked next investments with revenue gain, ROI, cost, speed

**What you collect:** goals, data they're willing to share, then continue. Do not start with "what's your budget?"

**Pricing, later:** three options (faster / slower / more channels). Goal is to **pay for the retainer inside 3–6 months**. No public rate card. Hours are a last-resort translation, not the SKU.

**Guarantee-shaped language (only with real policy):**

- Add resources free if pacing behind
- Remove a service that cannot hit the goal
- When goals hit, show where the next dollar goes

## Site map (keep it this small)

| URL job | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `/` | Outcome positioning + three differentiators + plan CTA |
| `/marketing-plan` | The entire sales funnel |
| `/services` | Puzzle pieces under one roof + named recipes |
| `/services/[channel]` | Same skeleton, channel-specific proof and checklist |
| `/pricing` | Not a grid — a plan form plus "what's in the plan" |
| `/results` | Industrial proof, indexed by objection |
| `/[software]` | Ops product as a reason they can be proactive |
| Recipe URLs (`/ppc/…`, `/cro/…`) | Thought leadership that names the tactic |

`/expertise` as vapor ("Coming soon") is a miss. Do not copy that.

## Homepage skeleton

1. **Sticky bar** — current goal-hit rate + CTA
2. **Hero** — Outcome H1, accountability H2, one CTA, data types you'll use (financial, marketing, CS, competitor, market)
3. **Proof density** — "more case studies than they have clients," logos, G2/Clutch
4. **Creative proof** — named campaign series, not a generic portfolio
5. **Differentiator 1: goals** — KPI% vs time%, kill what doesn't work, scale what does
6. **Differentiator 2: money math** — interactive grid of next-best spend
7. **Differentiator 3: recipes** — published tactics + show (podcast/YouTube)
8. **Lead magnet recap** — five data layers in the free plan
9. **Team** — specialists by function (paid, CRO, SEO, creative, BI). Culture is proof of care.
10. **Software** — 24/7 anomaly and opportunity tracking

Every section loops the same CTA.

## Service page skeleton

1. Channel H1 that still talks money
2. Bundle statement: this channel + CRO + attribution, not the channel alone
3. "We find more improvements in 5 minutes than most teams do in 5 weeks" + plan CTA
4. Sub-services (search, social, shopping, etc.)
5. Named recipe links
6. Case-study PDFs / logos
7. Giant checklist of included work (make scope tangible)
8. FAQ
9. Adjacent-channel upsell (once the page converts, what else)

Engagement menu to offer:

- Ongoing management (default)
- Audit
- Consulting (they execute, you prioritize)
- One-time build
- Pure performance (longer commitment, pay on lift)

## Results page skeleton

Do not dump testimonials in one chronological blob.

1. Hero with the average timeboxed result
2. Video proof (quantity as a joke, then the videos)
3. Filterable case studies
4. **Objection index:** results, expertise, cost, speed, proactivity, reporting, extra mile, honesty, scale, savings, quality, data, education, fun, vs other agencies, **bad things**, hail-mary
5. Four reviews per objection, then "there's more"
6. Include a "we're not perfect" section with real complaints (billing glitch, missing capability, over-initiative)

This page exists to exhaust skepticism. Match the buyer's fear to a review cluster.

## Landing page for paid traffic

Paid click → dedicated page. Homepage is for brand and sales, not for SKAGs.

Must-haves from their landing-page checklist (apply, don't paste):

- Conversion research before design
- Breadcrumb / multi-step for high-ask forms
- FAQ hierarchy
- Single-variable tests when volume is low; multivariate only with traffic
- Message match and traffic-intent
- Confirmation-page next step
- ICE-prioritized backlog
- Multi-device, location variants if the offer needs them
- Attention ratio (strip nav, one CTA)
- Social proof researched for that ICP, not generic logos
- Hero image that matches the ad

## Growth grid (how to present next spend)

Rank options by **revenue ROI and time-to-impact**, not by which channel the team likes.

Typical order they teach (illustrative math, replace with real client numbers):

1. Onsite CRO (often highest ROI, weeks not quarters)
2. Dedicated landing pages
3. SEO (slower, still on the grid)
4. More Google spend (show CPA bleed-up)
5. More LinkedIn spend (usually worse ROI, still listed so the client sees why)

Let the buyer toggle the north-star KPI (leads vs SQLs vs revenue vs profit) and watch the ranking change.

## Software story (optional, only if true)

If you have ops tooling, sell it as: goal tracking, ICP-quality traffic (not just CPC), alerts on pacing, and an audit queue sorted by impact. Do not invent a product. If you don't have one, describe the **cadence** (weekly 10–20 min, QBR, anomaly review) instead.
