# Named tactics

Name the recipe. Teach it in one screen. Link a deeper post if you publish one.

Use these as **principles**. Platform UIs change; the discrepancy problem does not. Re-validate match types and Shopping settings against current Google docs before implementing.

## Always-on pairing

**Ads without a dedicated landing page is an incomplete job.** Granular traffic (SKAGs, audiences, Shopping segments) is wasted if the page is a homepage. After structure is clean, the next leap is post-click: message match, one CTA, Breadcrumb forms, ICE tests.

## PPC structure

### Iceberg Effect

The keyword you see is the tip. The search terms you pay for sit underwater. A fat iceberg = high search-term-to-keyword discrepancy = wrong traffic, weak control, junk CPA.

**Fix:** drive the ratio toward 1:1. Isolate keywords, scan search terms, negative out junk, stop stuffing 10–20 keywords into one ad group.

### SKAGs (Single Keyword Ad Groups)

One root keyword per ad group. Historically: phrase + exact in the same ad group (not raw broad). Name the ad group after the keyword. Put the keyword in the ad. Refine long-tails into new SKAGs and negative them from the parent.

**Why it works:** message match, higher CTR → Quality Score → cheaper clicks, clearer CRO hypotheses because you know who arrived.

**Cost:** more ad groups, slower ad tests, more negatives. Test on the top keywords first.

**Today:** keep the *granularity and message-match* idea. RSA headline diversity and close-variant behavior mean you should not cargo-cult 2014 Editor workflows. Still refuse Google's "10–20 keywords per ad group" default.

### Keyword Tapering

A mining campaign (broader match) exists only to **find** converting queries. Winners get promoted into exact/isolated campaigns. Volume in the mine tapers down. Budget concentrates on the winners-circle campaign.

### Bottom Feeding

Only after core campaigns plateau. Add **all** plausible audiences in Observation, keep the ~10–15% that show efficient CPA, then a new campaign in Targeting with **broad** keywords constrained by those audiences. Purpose: catch leftover queries your tight SKAGs filtered out.

**Do not** start a new account here. Copy still has to convert; this is volume after control.

### SPAGs (Single Product Ad Groups)

Shopping analog of SKAGs: isolate products so you can bid and diagnose at SKU level.

### Mob Effect (Shopping)

Unsegmented Shopping dumps budget on generic research queries ("flat screen tvs") while SKU-level queries ("Samsung MU6070 50 inch") starve. The mob spends your money.

### Gold Pan Technique (Shopping)

Sift intent: **generic / brand / SKU** campaigns, priority settings, shared negative lists, differentiated bids, **shared budget** so they stay in the same auction. Generic is the pan; brand and SKU catch the gold. Feed quality is the deposit.

## CRO and pages

### Breadcrumb Technique

For **high-ask** conversions (quote, demo, consult), do the opposite of "cut fields."

1. Step 1: easy, anonymous, low-threat questions (the visitor stays unknown).
2. That micro-yes commits them (foot-in-the-door / yes ladder).
3. Last step: name, email, phone.

Field **order** beats field **count**. "Company name" can be too identifying for step 1. Low-ask offers (ebook, webinar) usually do **not** need this.

Reported pattern from their public tests: splitting a long one-step form into breadcrumbs produced order-of-magnitude CPA drops on lead-gen pages. Re-test; do not cite their numbers as yours.

### Attention ratio

Page elements that can be clicked vs the one conversion action. Aim near **1:1** on paid LPs: kill nav, secondary links, and competing campaigns.

### Message match

Search / ad / headline / hero image should feel like one sentence. SKAGs make this possible; homepages break it.

### ICE backlog

Score every test: Impact, Confidence, Ease. Run the high scores. Two-week max. Isolate one variable unless traffic supports multivariate. Split-URL for heavy redesigns.

## Email and outbound

### TLC (Transactional, Lifecycle, Content/promo)

Three email pillars, not a blast calendar:

- **Transactional:** receipt, tracking, feedback — highest open rates; seed next product; don't be greedy on email 1
- **Lifecycle:** welcome, browse/cart abandon, upsell, win-back — tied to buyer stage
- **Content & promo:** launches, urgency, subscriber-only, seasonal, UGC — segmented by AOV / discount-seekers / face-value buyers

Abandoned cart: value in emails 1–2, **incentive only on the last** so you don't train people to wait for a coupon.

### Heat Ladder (cold outbound)

Do not start with a bought list. Work hottest leftover demand first:

1. Previous customers
2. Lost opportunities (qualified)
3. Lost leads (not yet qualified)
4. Intent / reverse-IP
5. Engaged newsletter
6. Competitor-review switchers
7. Net-new list (last)

## Experiment ops

Three KPI layers: company north star → channel KPI → experiment KPI. Leadership will never care that LP CVR went up unless leads/revenue moved. Tie the story up the chain.

High tempo, small blast radius: 10–20% of traffic when the change is scary; two weeks, then decide.
